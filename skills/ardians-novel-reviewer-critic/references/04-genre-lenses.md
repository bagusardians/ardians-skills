# Genre Lenses

Load the lens that matches the work before scoring. A lens states the genre's reader
promise, the conventions that must be met or deliberately broken, and the criteria that
matter more in this genre than in the default rubric. Judge against the promise, and
reward intentional, effective subversion; penalize accidental failure.

If a book straddles genres, load both lenses and say which promise dominates and which
conventions are being fused.

## How to use a lens

1. State the promise in one sentence: what did the reader pick this up to feel or get?
2. Check the must-haves. Missing must-haves cap several categories regardless of prose
   quality.
3. Apply the weighted emphasis: raise the weight of the categories this genre lives on.
4. Note deliberate rule-breaking and judge whether it pays off.

## Fantasy

- **Promise:** wonder, a coherent invented world, and stakes that matter within it.
- **Must-haves:** consistent magic or world rules; a cost to power; a world that reacts to
  the protagonist's choices.
- **Emphasis:** worldbuilding (raise to ~15%), story, character.
- **Common failures:** rules broken for convenience; magic with no cost; a quest with no
  interior arc; exposition delivered as history lecture.
- **Series note:** a first volume must satisfy on its own while leaving a hook.

## Science fiction

- **Promise:** a speculative idea taken seriously, and its human consequence.
- **Must-haves:** an internally consistent premise; the idea changes lives, not just
  scenery; a character who must reckon with it.
- **Emphasis:** concept and originality (raise to ~15%), worldbuilding, theme.
- **Common failures:** the idea is explained but never dramatized; science-as-decor;
  characters serve as mouthpieces; the novum has no social consequence.
- **Hard-SF note:** physics and logic must hold; soft-SF must hold its own stated logic.

## Mystery and crime

- **Promise:** a puzzle that is fair, and a solution that satisfies.
- **Must-haves:** clues planted before the reveal; a detective with agency; a solution
  reachable in hindsight.
- **Emphasis:** story and plot (raise to ~20%), structure, character.
- **Common failures:** withheld clues; a culprit introduced late; coincidence as
  detection; motive that appears only at the reveal.
- **Fair-play test:** reread the reveal and confirm every necessary clue was on the page.

## Thriller and suspense

- **Promise:** mounting tension and a threat that feels real.
- **Must-haves:** jeopardy to something valued; a ticking clock or rising pressure; a
  competent opposing force.
- **Emphasis:** pacing (raise to ~15%), story, emotional impact.
- **Common failures:** tension reset after each beat; a villain who is only a label;
  protagonists who survive by authorial convenience; sagging second act.
- **Escalation test:** each act must raise the cost; if act two costs less than act one,
  it sags.

## Romance

- **Promise:** a central love story with real obstacles and an emotionally earned
  resolution.
- **Must-haves:** two leads with agency; a credible obstacle; chemistry shown, not
  asserted; an HEA or HFN the story earns.
- **Emphasis:** character (raise to ~20%), emotional impact (raise to ~15%), dialogue.
- **Common failures:** conflict resolved by a misunderstanding cleared in one
  conversation; insta-love with no obstacle; the leads are kept apart by contrivance.
- **Beat check:** the meet, the barrier, the dark moment, and the resolution must each
  change the relationship.

## Horror

- **Promise:** dread and the transgression of a boundary, sustained.
- **Must-haves:** a source of dread with rules; escalating threat; a cost to survival;
  atmosphere built through specific detail.
- **Emphasis:** emotional impact (raise to ~18%), worldbuilding, prose.
- **Common failures:** gore substituting for dread; the threat explained so fully it stops
  being frightening; characters making stupid choices to enable scares.
- **Dread test:** the scariest moment should be before the monster is fully seen.

## Literary fiction

- **Promise:** interior truth, language as pleasure, and a resonance that outlasts plot.
- **Must-haves:** a controlling intelligence; prose that rewards attention; a character
  whose inner life is the real subject.
- **Emphasis:** prose and voice (raise to ~18%), character, theme.
- **Common failures:** beautiful sentences without forward motion; theme stated in
  epiphany; ambiguity mistaken for depth; a plot too thin to carry the length.
- **Integration test:** theme, character, and language should be one thing, not three.

## Historical fiction

- **Promise:** immersion in a past that feels lived, plus a human story that fits it.
- **Must-haves:** accurate period detail that serves character; a story whose stakes are
  native to the era; no anachronistic sensibility unless deliberate.
- **Emphasis:** worldbuilding (raise to ~15%), character, theme.
- **Common failures:** museum-tour description; modern attitudes wearing period clothes;
  a history lecture with a thin plot attached.
- **Accuracy note:** flag factual issues with sources, using `evidence-based-citations`
  when claims are checkable. Never invent historical facts.

## Young adult

- **Promise:** a young protagonist's agency and first-time intensity, told at an
  accessible reading level.
- **Must-haves:** a teen protagonist driving the plot; stakes that matter to a teen; an
  age-appropriate reading level; no adult interference solving the problem.
- **Emphasis:** character (raise to ~18%), pacing, emotional impact.
- **Common failures:** teen voice that reads as an adult's memory of being young; adults
  who save the day; length and pacing that lose the target reader.
- **Voice test:** the narration should be a teen's present, not nostalgia for it.

## Comedy and satirical fiction

- **Promise:** laughs earned by character and structure, with a target or a truth.
- **Must-haves:** comedy that arises from character; escalation; a tonal center that holds
  even when the plot turns serious.
- **Emphasis:** dialogue (raise to ~15%), prose, structure.
- **Common failures:** jokes that contradict character; a premise stretched past its
  engine; tonal whiplash into unearned seriousness.
- **Landing-rate test:** count the intended jokes and the ones that land; the ratio, not
  volume, is the score.

## Middle grade

- **Promise:** a child protagonist solving a child-sized problem with warmth and clarity.
- **Must-haves:** a protagonist the reader's age; a problem they solve themselves; a clear,
  warm narrative voice; a satisfying, safe landing.
- **Emphasis:** character, prose (clarity and read-aloud quality), theme.
- **Common failures:** a problem too dark for the age; vocabulary above level; an adult
  resolving the arc.
- **Read-aloud test:** read a page aloud; if it stumbles, the prose score drops.

## Cross-genre and genre-blend notes

- Name the dominant promise and the secondary lens. If the blend is deliberate, judge each
  promise on its own terms without penalizing the fusion.
- If the blend is accidental and the two promises conflict, that is a positioning problem:
  state which promise the book actually keeps and recommend the author commit to it.
