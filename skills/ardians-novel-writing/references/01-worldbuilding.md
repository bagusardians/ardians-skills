# Phase 1: Worldbuilding

Worldbuilding happens immediately after the premise is agreed and before style, voice, or
drafting. A story is built on a world; if the world is undefined, every later phase invents
it by accident. This phase composes `brainstorming` to generate options and
`technical-writing` to write them down cleanly.

## Why this phase comes first

The premise names a situation. The world explains what that situation *means*: what is
possible, what is forbidden, who holds power, what a person can lose, and what the reader
should feel is at stake. Locking the world early prevents continuity drift in drafting and
gives the style and voice phases something concrete to speak through.

## Decisions to lock

- **Place and period.** Where and when the story happens, at the level of detail the story
  will actually use. Real, invented, or blended.
- **Scale and scope.** One room, one city, one country, one cosmos. State the boundary of
  what the world contains and what lies outside it.
- **Rules.** Physical, social, technological, magical, or legal rules and their limits.
  State explicitly what is impossible, and why the impossibility matters.
- **Institutions.** The organisations, trades, families, or systems that constrain
  characters. Who holds power, and how it is enforced.
- **Cost and stakes.** What a character can lose: status, safety, memory, a name, a
  livelihood, a person. Stakes must be world-legible, not asserted.
- **Texture.** Sensory and material anchors the prose can return to: architecture, food,
  money, clothing, weather, sound, smell.
- **Vocabulary.** Names for real things in this world, and forbidden anachronisms or
  register breaks.

## Steps

1. Brainstorm two or three candidate worlds against the premise. For each, state in one
   sentence what the world makes hard for the protagonist.
2. Choose one. Write the world bible as testable rules, not adjectives.
3. Name the three or four texture anchors the prose will return to.
4. State what the world must never do (the tonal or logical failures to avoid).
5. Record open questions and carry them to the research phase.

## Deliverable

A world bible containing places, period, rules and limits, institutions, stakes, texture
anchors, terminology, and forbidden patterns.

## Gate

Present the world bible. Ask the user to approve or revise before phase 2. Do not start
drafting, and do not lock style or voice, until the world is agreed.

## Checklist

- Every decision above has an answer.
- Rules are testable and have stated limits.
- At least three texture anchors are named.
- What is impossible is stated, with the reason it matters.
- Open questions are carried forward, not silently dropped.
